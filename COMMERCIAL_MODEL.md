# 2. Commercial thesis

Regstead is intended to be a low-friction recurring-revenue product.

The core commercial proposition is:

> **A one-off audit tells you what is there today. Regstead remembers it and tells you when something important changes later.**

The customer is paying primarily for:

- continuous observation;
- memory of the previous state;
- evidence-backed change detection;
- regulatory context;
- reduced manual checking;
- early warning when important public information breaks, disappears or changes materially;
- a usable history of what Regstead observed.

The recurring value is therefore much stronger than a one-off scan alone.

# 3. Initial customer profile

Primary launch market:

**Independent and regional SRA-regulated law firms in England and Wales.**

Ideal initial customer:

- approximately 1–10 offices;
- provides at least one service covered by SRA Transparency Rules;
- commonly provides conveyancing, probate/private client, employment, immigration, motoring, licensing or debt recovery;
- has a meaningful public website containing pricing, regulatory, complaints, document, form or quote surfaces;
- does not have a large internal website/compliance engineering team;
- can make a small SaaS purchasing decision without enterprise procurement.

Primary buyer roles:

- Managing Partner;
- Practice Manager;
- Operations Manager;
- COLP / COFA;
- Compliance Manager;
- occasionally Marketing/Digital where they own the website.

# 5. Pricing analysis

## Why £9.95 is appropriate for the founding cohort

£9.95 is intentionally a **validation price**, not the long-term economic centre of Regstead.

At this level the decision for a small firm is deliberately easy.

The objective of the first 25 customers is to learn:

- will firms actually pay;
- do they understand the proposition;
- do they remain subscribed;
- which findings they value;
- which alerts they ignore;
- how much support they require;
- whether reports are understandable;
- whether Regstead finds enough meaningful changes to justify recurring monitoring;
- whether customers recommend it.

The first 25 customers are therefore primarily a **product-validation asset**.

### Revenue from 25 founding customers

25 × £9.95:

**MRR: £248.75**

**ARR: £2,985.00**

That is not enough revenue to justify the business by itself.

It is enough to prove that strangers are willing to pay for the product.

That distinction is important.

---

# 6. Why the founding offer should stop at 25

The founding programme should remain scarce.

Do not keep extending £9.95 because it is easier to sell.

Twenty-five customers is enough to provide:

- multiple real website architectures;
- real monitoring history;
- feedback across different firm sizes;
- early retention data;
- testimonials/case studies where permitted;
- evidence of support load;
- evidence of alert usefulness;
- a meaningful conversion signal.

After the first 25, new customers should normally pay the public price.

Otherwise the founding price becomes the actual market price and is difficult to unwind.

# 8. What 100 customers actually means

100 customers is an important validation milestone, but at the current low-price model it is **not yet a large SaaS business**.

### 100 customers all paying £19

MRR:

**£1,900**

ARR:

**£22,800**

### More realistic early mix

25 founders at £9.95 plus 75 Standard customers at £19:

MRR:

**£1,673.75**

ARR:

**£20,085**

This would be strong evidence of product-market demand, but it would still be relatively modest revenue if Regstead required substantial manual support.

Therefore the product must become operationally efficient.

The economics work best when:

- scans are automated;
- reports are generated automatically;
- alerts are automated;
- onboarding is largely self-service;
- false positives remain low;
- customer support requirements are small.

# 10. The price-volume trade-off

At £19/month:

- 100 customers = £22,800 ARR
- 500 customers = £114,000 ARR
- 1,000 customers = £228,000 ARR

Therefore £19 works very well as an accessible entry plan, but it may be too low to be the only long-term plan.

Regstead should ultimately increase average revenue per account through **real additional value**, not arbitrary price inflation.

Likely mechanisms:

- £39 Plus plan;
- multi-domain/site plans;
- higher monitoring frequency;
- additional vertical/rule packs;
- group/multi-office management;
- richer alert routing;
- agency/compliance-consultant accounts;
- premium evidence/reporting features.

Do not build these before the core monitoring proposition is validated.

# 11. Initial commercial validation target

Current recommended validation experiment:

### Qualified firms approached

**50**

These should be properly selected prospects, not bulk cold-email volume.

Each should receive:

- a fresh scan;
- manual review of the result;
- a truthful personalised observation or monitoring angle;
- an offer of the baseline report.

### Desired outcomes

**10+ meaningful conversations**

and

**5+ paying customers**

would be a strong initial signal.

### Caution threshold

If only **0–1 customers pay after 50 high-quality approaches**, stop and investigate before materially increasing development spend.

Possible causes:

- wrong customer;
- weak proposition;
- poor messaging;
- insufficient perceived risk/value;
- wrong price;
- product output not useful enough;
- monitoring cadence/value unclear.

Do not automatically respond by adding more features.

# 14. Indicative retention goals

These are working targets, not promises.

During founding beta:

- understand every cancellation;
- manually interview or request feedback from lost founders where appropriate;
- aim for high 90-day retention;
- do not obscure churn with long contracts.

A customer who cancels because Regstead produced little recurring value is more informative than a customer held by a contract.

# 15. Customer acquisition strategy

Initial acquisition should be highly personalised.

Preferred channel:

1. identify suitable firm;
2. run a fresh scan;
3. inspect results;
4. identify one real observation;
5. email an appropriate decision-maker;
6. offer the baseline report;
7. follow up politely;
8. invite to founding programme.

Avoid:

- bulk spam;
- fake urgency;
- claims of non-compliance;
- fear-based subject lines;
- invented findings;
- implying affiliation with the SRA.

The strongest sales message is likely to be:

> **Your website may be correct today. Regstead is designed to tell you when that changes.**

# 16. Sales proposition hierarchy

Do not lead with:

> “Compliance software.”

Prefer:

### First
**Continuous monitoring of important website information.**

### Second
**Regstead remembers what was previously published.**

### Third
**It understands the regulatory surfaces that matter to your type of business.**

### Fourth
**It gives evidence-backed alerts when something changes.**

The regulatory context adds value.

The recurring memory is the core product distinction.

# 18. Manual work during beta

Some manual review during the founding stage is acceptable.

The purpose is to learn.

However, record manual time.

Tasks that repeatedly require manual work should be candidates for:

- automation;
- improved detectors;
- better discovery;
- clearer product boundaries.

Do not quietly turn Regstead into a £9.95/month consultancy service.

---

# 19. Cost discipline

Until customer demand is proven:

- keep hosting simple;
- avoid premature enterprise architecture;
- avoid unnecessary paid AI;
- avoid expensive third-party monitoring platforms;
- avoid large advertising spend;
- avoid building integrations without customer demand.

Spend first on things that improve:

- finding accuracy;
- reliability;
- security;
- customer acquisition;
- payment collection;
- customer trust.

---

# 20. When to revisit pricing

Revisit public pricing when one or more of these occurs:

- 25 founding places are filled;
- 50+ Standard customers are paying;
- strong evidence shows customers would pay more;
- support cost makes £19 unattractive;
- Plus features have emerged naturally;
- additional verticals create materially different value;
- multi-domain/group customers request more capability.

Pricing should be changed for a reason.

# 23. Commercial decision gates

## Gate 1 — technical trust

Before paid launch:

- Milestone 5 demonstrates useful accuracy improvements;
- severe false positives remain extremely rare;
- reports are understandable;
- scans are reproducible.

## Gate 2 — willingness to pay

After 50 high-quality approaches:

- target >=5 paying customers.

If not achieved, investigate before building extensively.

## Gate 3 — retention

After founding customers have sufficient monitoring history:

- assess 30/60/90-day retention;
- understand cancellations;
- validate recurring rather than one-off value.

## Gate 4 — scale

Before aggressively acquiring hundreds of customers:

- scheduled scanning automated;
- report generation automated;
- alerting automated;
- payment/billing automated;
- customer support load understood;
- monitoring cost understood.

---

# 24. Commercial north star

Regstead should become:

> **A low-friction subscription that a regulated small business keeps because it quietly watches something important that nobody wants to check manually every week.**

The product should be valuable enough to retain customers, inexpensive enough to be an easy purchasing decision, and automated enough that hundreds of customers can be served without hundreds of hours of manual work.
